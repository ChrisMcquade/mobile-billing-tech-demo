Billing · Digital channel

The biggest reason customers message us is a number they don’t understand.

Billing is the largest contact driver in messaging, and almost all of it is comprehension: what a charge is, why the bill moved, what happens next. Very little of it needs an agent to change anything — it needs the number explained.

Containment = the conversation resolved in messaging without handing to a human agent.

Why voice plus generative UI

Voice is fast, but a bill is a table.

Reading a nine-line breakdown aloud is worse than saying nothing. The agent talks; the screen carries the detail.

The customer is already in the app.

The bill they are asking about is on the device in their hand. The agent works on top of it, not in a separate window.

Comprehension is the whole job.

Show the number, show what moved, show the evidence. Most billing contacts end there.

How the customer gets here

Two entry points, sequenced. The first proves the mechanism against traffic we already have. The second only earns its place once containment is real.

1

Entry point 1 — from messaging

Phase 1 · live in this demo

The customer is already messaging. The NLU classifies a billing intent. Rather than queue for a human, they are offered a deep link that opens the bills screen in the app and starts the voice agent, carrying what they already said so nothing is repeated.

2

Entry point 2 — from the billing screens

Phase 2 · after containment is proven

Tactical starts placed directly in the billing screens: an Explain my bill action at the foot of a bill, a Query this charge action on an individual line. Same agent, same components, no chat needed to get in. Marked on the demo screen but deliberately inert.

What the agent puts on screen

The agent does not generate interface code. It picks a component from a catalogue this page declares and supplies a JSON payload matching that component’s schema. The page renders it with components we own, styled to brand. Anything the agent sends that does not match a schema is rejected and the agent is told why — including the field, the reason, and the list of components it may actually use.

The four components the agent may reference, what each shows, and when it is used.
ComponentWhat it showsWhen the agent uses it
bill_summary_cardScripted The whole bill in one card: total, whether it has been taken, what moved since last month, and where the money went. The first answer to any “what is my bill” or “why has it changed” question.
usage_allowance_cardScripted An allowance against what was used, the date it was crossed, and how the extra was calculated. When the customer queries an out-of-plan or over-allowance charge.
bill_period_selector A short list of recent bills, each with its amount and status. When it is not clear which bill the customer means.
charge_detail_card One charge on its own: amount, when it applied, and whether it recurs. When a single line needs explaining and the full bill would be noise.

Notes and limitations

  • Mock data only. Figures are illustrative and are not O2 pricing. The account number, the device and the usage are invented for the demo.
  • One demo account, one scripted path. The agent will answer off-script but has no data beyond the fixture, so it will generalise rather than look anything up.
  • Phase 2 entry points are shown but inert. They are marked on the bills screen to show placement, not to be used.
  • No payments, no card or bank details, no document sending. Those are out of scope for the demo by design, not pending work.
  • Containment figures are indicative. They describe the shape of the opportunity and are not a forecast.
  • The component catalogue is fixed by this page. The agent cannot invent a component or a field; anything outside the schema is rejected and returned to the agent as a structured error.
  • Voice quality depends on the device and the room. In a noisy stakeholder session, use the scripted mode.
